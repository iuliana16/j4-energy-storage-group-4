# Story 4: Chemical / Power-to-X – green ammonia at Ny-Ålesund

*Author:* Charlie Thomson · *Site:* Ny-Ålesund, Svalbard, Norway (off-grid, diesel-powered Arctic research settlement, no mainland grid connection) · *System:* 1.7 MW electrolyser, Haber-Bosch ammonia synthesis with air separation, about 500 m³ pressurised ammonia tank and ammonia-fuelled generator set (genset), delivering 680 MWh of electricity each polar night

*Own model (not from a source):* polar-night demand about 1,360 MWh, read off [1] Fig. 2.2; storage target 680 MWh, 50% of polar-night demand (design assumption); electrolyser runs 1,500 full-load hours a year (design assumption); about 286 t of ammonia a year; round-trip efficiency about 25%; capital cost (CAPEX) about USD 3.89 million (€3.50 million) for the electrolyser and synthesis plant only, excluding the tank and genset; operation and maintenance (O&M) €105,000 a year; levelised cost of storage (LCOS) €636/MWh and net present value (NPV) −€3.15 million at an 8% discount rate over 25 years; tank steel about 76 t (own wall-thickness sizing at 18 bar and 140 MPa allowable stress); diesel avoided about 270 litres per MWh (assumes about 37% generator efficiency); 2.68 kg CO₂ per litre of diesel (assumed standard combustion factor); emissions avoided 724 kg CO₂/MWh; global warming potential (GWP) 8.6 kg CO₂e/MWh (tank steel only); low and high ranges of ±10–30% are assumptions. All results were recalculated and passed by the group's verification agent, including a 10,000-case Monte Carlo uncertainty analysis.
